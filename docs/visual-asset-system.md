# Artisan House LLC — Job Photo Style Guide & Visual Asset System

*Built from a representative analysis of ~108 real Artisan House job photos (2020–2026) across basements, bathrooms, kitchens, exteriors, decks/porches, pergolas, pools, stonework, ADA ramps, and structural inspections. Cohesive with the existing brand system: **Bebas Neue** (display) + **DM Sans** (body), and the red / wood / gold / ink / bone palette.*

## Part 1 — What your photos actually look like (the honest read)

Consistent patterns across the whole library:

**Subject matter (your real service mix, by frequency in the sample):**
1. **Basement finishing** — your single largest, most complete story (before → demo → framing → drywall → paint → finished living rooms). Signature: gray/greige walls, recessed cans, warm wood-look floors, white support columns.
2. **Bathroom remodels** — very frequent, tile-heavy. Herringbone & subway tile, marble-look large format, mosaic niches, black matte fixtures, gold hardware, floating vanities.
3. **Kitchens** — two-tone cabinets (navy/white, sage/gray), quartz/marble counters, waterfall islands, black-stainless appliances.
4. **Exteriors & craftsmanship carpentry** — timber-frame porches, cedar pergolas, decks, **ADA accessibility ramps** (a genuine differentiator), paver hardscaping, stacked-stone veneer columns.
5. **Pools** — liner replacement/restoration (a newer, growing service).
6. **Inspections / structural** — framing, joist sistering, mold/moisture documentation.

**The craftsmanship signature** — warm natural wood (cedar, oak, walnut) against cool modern gray/white finishes. That warm-wood-on-cool-neutral contrast IS your visual brand, and it maps perfectly onto the logo palette (wood + ink + bone + gold).

**Lighting:** Interiors lean on recessed cans + window daylight — bright but often flat, occasionally dim/uneven with hotspots from bare fixtures. Your **exterior shots are consistently your strongest** — clean overcast light or warm golden hour.

**Color/white balance:** Currently **inconsistent** — older (2020–2021) shots run warm/yellow; newer ones neutral-to-cool. There is **no unified tone yet**. Fixing this is the single biggest lever for a premium, cohesive look (see Part 3).

## Part 2 — Job Photo Style Guide (how to shoot going forward)

Goal: make every new photo look like it belongs to the same premium brand, with zero extra gear — just a phone, shot consistently.

**Setup (once):**
- Samsung camera → Settings → turn **OFF** the "Galaxy watermark" / date stamp.
- Turn **ON** gridlines and the level indicator. Shoot **horizontal (landscape)** for heroes and galleries; vertical only for tall detail (tile columns, doors, stairwells).
- Wipe the lens. Shoot at the **main (1×) lens**, not ultrawide (kills the wide-angle bulge on rooms).

**Framing:**
- **Rooms:** stand in a corner, phone at **chest height (~4–4.5 ft)**, back level. One-point or two-point perspective down the longest sightline. Keep verticals vertical.
- **Detail shots:** fill the frame with the craftsmanship — a tile niche, a mitered joint, wood grain, hardware. These are gold for cards and backgrounds and you don't shoot enough of them.
- **Exteriors:** shoot the **3/4 angle** (front + one side), on overcast days or the **golden hour** (first/last 90 min of sun). Avoid harsh noon.

**The 10-second "after" ritual** (biggest quality jump):
1. Remove tools, buckets, cords, drop cloths, and **yellow tile spacers** from frame.
2. Turn on all the lights (recessed + vanity/accent).
3. Open blinds halfway (light in, but avoid full window blow-out).
4. Wipe reflective surfaces, peel appliance stickers.
5. Shoot **3 frames**: one wide establishing, one medium (the feature — island, vanity, fireplace), one tight detail.

**Before/after discipline:** shoot the **"before" from the exact same spot and angle** you'll shoot the "after." Matched pairs are your most persuasive marketing asset and you currently have very few true matches.

**Consistency target:** bright, **neutral-to-warm** white balance, verticals straight, minimal clutter, warm wood reading warm and gray finishes reading clean (not yellow, not blue).

### Image treatment rules

**1. Global grade (apply to every published photo) — the brand "look":**
- White balance: pull toward **neutral-warm** (correct the sideways-cool and the old-yellow shots to meet in the middle).
- Exposure: +5–15% brightness on interiors; lift shadows slightly so wood stays rich but detail shows.
- Contrast: gentle S-curve. Saturation: **+5% max** (premium = restrained, never Instagram-punchy).
- Sharpening: light. Export sRGB.
- *One-line CSS approximation for quick consistency on already-decent photos:* `filter: brightness(1.05) contrast(1.03) saturate(1.04);`

**2. Text-over-image overlays (legibility + brand):**
- **Hero (text on photo):** bottom-up ink scrim
  `linear-gradient(to top, rgba(26,26,26,0.78) 0%, rgba(26,26,26,0.35) 40%, rgba(26,26,26,0) 70%)`
  Headline in Bebas Neue **Bone `#FAFAF7`**, with a **gold `#C9A24B` accent rule** under it.
- **Brand-color scrim (CTA bands):** Artisan Red at low opacity over a wood/texture photo:
  `linear-gradient(rgba(155,28,28,0.72), rgba(122,20,20,0.82))` — white text on top.
- **Never** put dark text directly on a busy photo. Always a scrim or a solid card.

**3. Duotones (for texture backgrounds, section dividers, icons):**
Convert a detail/texture shot to a two-tone image so it reads as brand texture, not a literal photo:
- **Ink → Bone** duotone for dark section backgrounds (wood grain, stone).
- **Walnut → Parchment** duotone for warm craft sections.
- Keep duotones at **10–20% opacity** behind text, or full strength as a thin divider strip.

**4. The "framed craft" motif (from your business card):**
Frame showcase photos with a **1px Walnut `#5E3A1E` border + subtle Parchment mat (8–12px padding)** and, on hover, a **gold `#C9A24B` hairline**. Echoes the framed look of your logo and reads high-end.

## Part 7 — Technical Recommendations

**Optimization**
- Serve **WebP** (or AVIF) with JPG fallback. Target: hero ≤ 250 KB, gallery thumb ≤ 60 KB, full ≤ 300 KB.
- Generate responsive sizes: 480 / 768 / 1280 / 1920 px wide; use `<img srcset sizes>` or `<picture>`.
- Your originals are **2–24 MB** — never ship those. Downscale longest edge to 1920px for full views, 800px for grid.
- **Strip EXIF** on export (removes GPS/location metadata — a privacy win) and **bake in rotation** (fixes the sideways problem permanently).
- Lazy-load everything below the fold: `loading="lazy"` + `decoding="async"`. Eager-load only the hero.

**Accessibility (alt text strategy)**
- Every content image needs descriptive alt: *"Two-tone navy and white kitchen remodel with quartz waterfall island, Medina Ohio."* Pattern: `[service] [key features], [city] Ohio`.
- Purely decorative duotones/textures: `alt=""` (skip them for screen readers).
- Don't stuff keywords; describe the actual scene. Maintain AA contrast on all text-over-image (the scrims in Part 3 are tuned for this).

**Responsive behavior**
- Art-direct heroes: a 16:9 crop on desktop, a tighter 4:5 crop on mobile (via `<picture>` + media queries) so the subject stays centered.
- Grids collapse 3-col → 2-col → 1-col. Keep tap targets ≥ 44px.

**Subtle animations (premium = restrained)**
- **Fade/slide-up on scroll** (IntersectionObserver, ~400ms, 20px rise) for gallery cards.
- **Hover zoom** on gallery images: `transform: scale(1.04)` over 300ms, `overflow:hidden` on the frame.
- **Before/after slider:** draggable, with an initial 1.5s auto-nudge to hint interactivity.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — disable non-essential motion.
- No autoplay carousels, no parallax on mobile (jank), no spinners longer than the image.
